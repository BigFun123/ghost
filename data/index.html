About Ghost

Ghost brings servo control, motion sequences and a 3D robot view together in your browser.

Configure your robot, explore movements in offline simulation, and monitor live controller and sensor metrics.

Capabilities

  • 3D view — Explore your robot assembly, inspect components and watch commanded movements from different angles.
  • Servo mixer — Set servo positions, adjust motion modes and speed, and control individual outputs with mute and solo.
  • Sequencer — Build, save and play repeatable motion sequences, with looping and adjustable playback speed.
  • Metrics — Follow servo angles and simulated sensor readings over time, choosing which measurements to display.
  • Physics — Explore simulated gravity and collisions, drop test objects and inspect collision shapes in the 3D view.
  • Offline simulation — Configure your robot and try movements in the browser without sending commands to hardware.
  • Reinforcement Learning (coming soon) — Train robot behaviours through trial and feedback in simulation.

Sim to real

Build and explore your robot in simulation, then use the same robot configuration, servo mixer and motion sequences to control its physical counterpart through a connected Ghost controller.

Map the simulated servos to the controller's physical outputs so movements in the app drive the matching servos on your real robot. Offline simulation lets you try movements first; when connected to hardware, the controls send commands to the robot.

Simulation is a starting point: check servo directions, movement limits and clearances on the physical robot, since real loads and motion can differ.

Warning: powerful servos

High-torque servos, including models sold as “150 kg” servos, can cause serious crushing and pinching injuries. A small movement can apply substantial force through a horn or linkage.

  • Keep hands, hair, clothing and tools clear of moving joints. Secure the robot and keep other people outside its movement area.
  • Before fitting horns, adjusting linkages or working on wiring, disconnect servo power and support any parts that could fall when torque is removed.
  • For first tests, use small movements and low speeds. Check directions, limits and clearances before running a sequence.
  • Keep a physical servo-power disconnect within reach. Browser controls, mute, output-off commands and a network connection are not a safety-rated emergency stop.
  • Use the manufacturer's specified supply voltage, suitable wiring and protection. Follow the safety instructions for your servo and power supply.

Simulation cannot guarantee safe behaviour on hardware. Treat the robot as capable of unexpected movement whenever servo power is connected.

        From a configured rig to a measured result

          Use Setup to select the PanTilt robot. The distance sensor must be attached beneath two enabled positional servos, with pan as the outer joint and tilt as the inner joint. Keep the target separate from the moving servo chain, above the floor and within reach of the sensor ray.

        02 / PATH TO SUCCESS
        Build holding first. Prove search separately.
        
          
        Parallel training grid: Select 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 64 or 256 Parallel environments in Policy Optimization. Sixteen displays a 4 x 4 grid; 64 and 256 display 8 x 8 and 16 x 16 grids of identical rigs, each in its own physics world with independent episode resets and seeded starts/targets. All robots share one policy. An update collects the selected steps from every environment before training; for example, 16 x 128 yields 2,048 samples per update. The single simulation telemetry follows E01; grid labels identify the other replicas. Pausing/stopping affects every replica. Display positions do not change policy inputs, sensor queries or physics. A single worker interleaves physics and batches network inference; this is vectorized training, not guaranteed multi-core or 16x speedup. Higher counts increase CPU physics, memory and optimizer work. Large grids use a simplified visual overview, fewer display updates and E01 episode logging (aggregate counters include every world); sensor geometry and simulation accuracy are unchanged. Evaluation always uses its separate reserved banks. The default remains one robot; saved weights work with any count, and loading restarts rollout/random sampler state.
        
          
        Reading Convergence: The big number is the latest complete monitoring success percentage, not a probability that the policy is optimal. Quality requires at least 90% success, a Wilson 95% lower bound of at least 80% (so at least 46/50 successes), zero invalid states and false holds. At least three distinct trained checkpoints must meet those rules, with success within 5 percentage points and successful-only median time/travel within 10% (floors: 0.1 s and 1 degree). Before that it reports insufficient evidence, improving/unstable, regressing or plateaued below target. Further training makes old evidence stale; repeated evaluation without training cannot create extra stability checkpoints. Loading/restoring marks archived evidence excluded, and rig/task changes require a new environment. Converged (validation) applies only to the selected clean simulation stage. Independent training seeds and physical robustness still require separate experiments.
        
          
        Full Auto Train: Choose the stage, update count, rollout length and batch limit, then press Full Auto Train. It initializes if needed and enables warm/progressive starts. After every batch it freezes weights, evaluates 50 monitoring cases and runs the startup-angle test (updating the test graph). The header displays that monitoring acquisition percentage, with a status and checkpoint count. Once three distinct trained checkpoints meet the quality and stability rules, a separate fresh 50-case final bank is compared with raster. Auto ends on final pass or failure; it does not repeatedly tune against that same final bank. Uncheck early stopping to continue to the budget before final qualification. At the limit it saves the best monitored checkpoint, which may still be unqualified; after qualification it saves current qualified weights. Stop auto train retains current weights for manual saving. Restore best monitored checkpoint restores weights with a fresh optimizer and marks archived evidence stale. With 100 x 4 x 128 and one environment the training budget is 51,200 action steps; multiply that by the environment count. Monitoring, probes and tests are additional.
        
          
        Use the budgets below to plan experiments, not to promise convergence. The current short browser runs have not demonstrated reliable ordinary-start acquisition. Each budget is additional training action steps for that stage; move on when the behavior passes its checkpoint, not simply when the counter reaches a number.

        What counts as a step?
        
          
        Training steps = Updates this run × Steps per rollout for a completed run. The default 4 × 128 is only 512 action steps. One action advances up to 50 ms of simulated time through six 120 Hz physics ticks; a full 10-second episode is about 200 actions. Optimizer minibatches and physics ticks are not extra action steps. An early terminal action can advance less than 50 ms.
        
          
        Repeated Train policy runs continue the retained weights; Run Steps describes the current run. Save separate checkpoints to track your total training budget. Partial runs can contain sampled steps or optimizer work without a complete update, so their totals may differ from the planned budget.
        
          
        How much real time should I allow?
        
          
        Use the measured steps/s in PPO Optimization: estimated seconds ≈ planned action steps ÷ measured steps/s. At 20 steps/s, 10,000 steps take about 8 minutes, 50,000 about 42 minutes, and 100,000 about 83 minutes. Your browser, backend and scene can change that rate; evaluation adds time. Training runs independently of render speed, while the interactive policy test is paced near real time.
        
          
        If progress stalls, diagnose before adding more steps
        
          
        If holding works but ordinary-start acquisition stays at zero, check reachability, episode duration, false holds and the gap between training and evaluation starts. Save the current policy, compare a fresh policy seed, and evaluate in measured batches. Flat results across several batches are a reason to revisit the curriculum, reward or exploration settings rather than assume a larger budget will fix them.
        
          
        Current limits: Warm start places training within roughly 0.25 degrees of the aiming pose; ordinary starts are around the configured startup angles (±5 degrees or ±20 degrees). The progression checkbox bridges those poses in six stages using frozen training-case probe outcomes. It requires at least 8 successes in 10 recent episodes to advance and steps back after poor performance. This is training guidance, not held-out convergence. Monitoring now uses 50 fixed cases per stage, and final qualification reserves a separate 50-case bank. Repeated monitoring is not new independent evidence. These clean-model cases do not yet cover occlusion, sensor noise, varied deadlines or independent training-seed replication; the estimates above are experimental planning ranges, not measured requirements or guarantees.

        04 / READING RESULTS
        A high reward is a clue. Acquisition is the test.
        
          
        What are held-out evaluations?
        
          
        Held-out means reserved from training. Think of training as practice exercises and held-out evaluation as an exam. The policy learns from one set of scenarios, then is tested on separate scenarios without changing its weights. This checks whether it can handle situations it did not practice.
        
          
        Here, a scenario is a repeatable starting setup identified by a seed (a number used to reproduce the setup). Configured-target stages test different starting angles; Random reachable targets also uses different target positions. The evaluation seeds are separate from the training seeds. Warm start is off during evaluation, even if it was on during training.
        
          
        How to evaluate between training batches
        
          
          
            
        1. Let the current Train policy run finish.
          2. 
            
        2. In Reference Scan, set Episode count to 5.
          3. 
            
        3. In Policy Optimization, keep the training stage you want to test and click Evaluate learned policy. This button uses the Policy Optimization stage and the Reference Scan episode count; the standalone raster stage/seed-split selections do not choose the paired policy tests.
          4. 
            
        4. Evaluate / convergence tests the frozen policy on all 50 monitoring cases. Final qualification, enabled after convergence, uses a separate 50-case bank and compares raster on the same final cases. No policy weights are updated.
          5. 
            
        5. Record the result, train another batch, then evaluate again under the same settings. 3/5 acquired means three test episodes successfully found and held the target; it does not mean the policy is 60% converged.
          6. 
          
        
          
        Monitoring reuses the same 50 reserved cases each checkpoint so progress is comparable. They are a validation set used for training decisions. The 95% Wilson interval is calculated from that checkpoint alone; repeating cases never increases its sample count. Final qualification uses another bank, with a new reproducible seed range per attempt. Bank identifiers and episode seeds are saved with policy data. Reopening an older checkpoint can repeat previously seen final cases, so preserve experiment provenance when judging unseen performance.
        
          
        
            
        Success & acquisition time
        Success means the selected target was held for the required time. Median acquisition uses successful episodes only. A dash means no successful acquisition was available. A timeout means the episode limit expired.
        
            
        Reward & losses
        Return combines detection, hold progress, alignment and completion rewards with time, movement and limit penalties. Alignment uses simulation geometry for training. Policy/value loss and entropy describe optimization; they do not prove that the robot learned to search.
        
            
        Travel & false returns
        Travel sums actual joint movement at 120 Hz, excluding reset settling. False returns count continuous stretches of valid readings from non-target surfaces at the 20 Hz observation rate. False holds count continuous Hold actions on those readings. Raster false locks count its separate lock attempts.
        
            
        Fair comparison
        The paired evaluation uses identical seeds, target positions, initial poses and time limits for both controllers. Compare success first, then acquisition time, return and travel. A partial comparison is unfinished evidence. Zero successes for both does not establish equivalence.
        
          
        
          
        Warm-start success demonstrates holding near an already aimed pose, not finding a target from an ordinary start. The policy receives joint angles, velocities, distance validity/range, recent history and the previous action. Target identity and position stay out of its observations.
        
        
        
        
        
          
        05 / WEIGHTS & POLICIES
        How learned numbers become robot behavior
        
          
        Weights are the adjustable numbers inside the neural network. Training changes these connection weights and biases so the network can choose useful actions from its observations. Think of them as thousands of tuning knobs whose combined settings shape behavior. A policy is the decision-making model; its weights are the learned part of that model.
        
          
        Watch the policy weight surface beside the training robots. Choose a weight matrix: observation to hidden 1, hidden 1 to hidden 2, the action head, or the value head. Rows and columns identify connected neurons; height and color show the signed weight. Blue means negative and amber means positive. Drag or use arrow keys to rotate. The shared policy has one set of weights for every training robot; biases are not plotted.
        
          
        The surface refreshes after optimizer updates, and when a policy is created, loaded or restored. Select Last update change to magnify differences from the previous snapshot; change RMS summarizes their size. A fresh or restored policy starts with zero change. Each layer keeps its height scale until values exceed it, so small changes remain comparable. Evaluation and policy tests freeze the weights. A changing surface shows learning activity; use held-out acquisition results and convergence to judge improvement.
        
          
        
            
        How the policy uses weights
        At each action step, joint angles, velocities, sensor distance/validity, recent history and the previous action become a 29-number observation. Two hidden layers combine those inputs using the weights. The network produces probabilities for Hold, Pan -, Pan +, Tilt - and Tilt +, plus a value estimate of expected future reward. The weights do not directly specify one fixed sequence of servo angles.
        
            
        How training changes weights
        The robot collects observations, actions and rewards in simulation. PPO uses those experiences to adjust the weights in small optimizer updates. During training, actions are sampled from the probabilities to support exploration. An action that helped in one episode does not become a hard-coded rule: learning depends on many experiences, and more training can improve or weaken earlier behavior.
        
            
        Testing and parallel robots
        Policy tests and evaluation keep weights frozen and choose the highest-probability action. They measure the current behavior without teaching the model. Every robot in a parallel training grid shares the same weights but supplies its own observations and resets independently. Their experiences feed one shared update; the grid does not create a different policy per robot.
        
            
        What Save and Load preserve
        Save policy stores the learned weights in the named JSON file, along with network/training settings, rig/task compatibility information and available evaluation evidence. Load policy restores compatible weights so the learned behavior can be tested or trained further. It restarts the optimizer, random sampler and rollout state, so loading is not an exact resume of an interrupted training session. Loaded convergence evidence is historical and needs fresh checks.
        
          
        
          
        The decision path: current observations → neural network using learned weights → action probabilities → selected action → bounded simulated servo movement. Privileged target identity and target position are not network inputs; rewards and evaluation use them separately to check the selected target.
        
          
        Reusing weights for a similar scenario
        
          
        Repeated Train policy runs continue the current weights. Starting from learned weights and adapting them is called fine-tuning. Keep a successful checkpoint, train further, and save a new version such as target-search-v2.json. Compare both old and new scenarios afterward: a higher training reward alone does not show that previous skills were retained.
        
          
        Same-rig target changes can reuse learned weights. Selecting a different target in Mission retains the policy and optimizer, rebuilds the isolated environment and enables Policy Test without another training run. Old test, monitoring and qualification results are cleared. After editing target positions or adding targets in Setup, initialize the updated environment and load your saved policy: target-only differences are allowed, while joint mapping/limits, sensor, action/observation format, reward and mission timing still must match. Save useful weights before editing Setup. Random reachable targets varies stationary target positions during training; moving-target tracking still needs additional implementation.
        
          
        Weights are not proof of success. A saved model may have learned to Hold everywhere or exploit an easy starting pose. Use Policy Test, monitoring and final qualification to establish what it can do before relying on that checkpoint.
        
        
        
        
        
          
        06 / SAVE & TROUBLESHOOT
        Keep your work and diagnose the next step
        
          
        Save policy writes a uniquely named JSON file to the project training/ folder on the devserver, or downloads a JSON file on other hosts. It contains weights, training settings, rig/schema compatibility data and available evaluation results. It is separate from robot configuration and sequence files.
        
          
        Load policy accepts a compatible JSON file up to 2 MB. Initialize the current rig and task first. Compatible target-only differences are allowed and clear prior evaluation evidence; other rig/task contract changes are rejected. Loading replaces weights and restarts the optimizer, sampling generator and rollout state; it is not an exact session resume. A rejected file leaves the current policy intact. Run a fresh evaluation after loading.
        
          
        Initialization says the rig cannot reach the target
        Check the selected joints and sensor ancestry, servo enabled state and positional mode, joint limits, target height and target placement. The target must not move with the sensor. A visible kinematic pose is a setup check; physical settling and the timed hold are tested in the isolated simulation.
        
          
        TensorFlow or WebGL fails to initialize
        Use the local development server for training assets. Try CPU in Episode configuration and initialize again. CPU may run more slowly. Read the event stream for the specific failure; the compute bar shows whether initialization succeeded.
        
          
        The sensor reports no hit or hits the floor
        The simulated sensor uses a single forward ray with a 2000 mm range. It reports the nearest surface, which may block the target. Orbit the 3D view, inspect the ray, and use bounded manual steps to check the aim. A valid distance alone does not mean the target was found.
        
          
        Training succeeds, but evaluation scores zero
        Warm-start training with progression off may have taught holding only. Enable progression to bridge toward ordinary starts. Check the policy test action counts: repeated Hold is a policy decision, not a stopped test. Policies trained before the mount-angle measurement correction must be retrained; incompatible saved files are rejected. Evaluate ordinary starts, train longer, and increase difficulty gradually. Compare with raster scanning. Do not treat a few updates or a higher shaped reward as proof of general target search.
        
          
        My saved policy is incompatible or my policy disappeared
        Restore compatible rig, joint mapping, limits and non-target task settings before loading. Selecting a different target or changing passive target geometry can transfer compatible weights; previous target-specific results are cleared. Initialization, validation, mission/robot/mode changes and page reload clear the in-memory session. Save useful policies before those actions. Clear in Event Stream removes log text only.
        
          
        This lab currently models a fixed base, stationary targets and a simplified sensor ray. Sensor noise, latency, broader generalization and transfer to a physical robot still need validation. Training or loading a policy does not upload anything to the controller.
